Every week, the wrestling conversation chases the newest belt change. Ethan Page won the Intercontinental Championship on Monday. The TBS Championship changed hands on Fright Night. Crown Jewel is two days away.

Meanwhile, the longest active reign WCN tracks keeps quietly growing. It isn't in WWE. It belongs to Jon Moxley.

Moxley won the AEW Continental Championship at Worlds End on December 27, 2025, by taking the Continental Classic final. As of today, that's **313 days**. No other champion on WCN's board across WWE, AEW, TNA, NXT and AAA is within 100 days of him.

Our take: this is the most underrated title run in wrestling right now, and the numbers show why.

## The gap is enormous

Here are the five longest active reigns WCN tracks, counted to November 5, 2026:

| Champion | Title | Won at | Since | Days |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jon Moxley | AEW Continental Championship | Worlds End | Dec 27, 2025 | 313 |
| Roman Reigns | World Heavyweight Championship (WWE) | WrestleMania 42 Night 2 | Apr 19, 2026 | 200 |
| Rey Fénix | AAA World Cruiserweight Championship | Noche de los Grandes | May 30, 2026 | 159 |
| Zaria | NXT Women's North American Championship | NXT | Jun 9, 2026 | 149 |
| The Vision | World Tag Team Championship (WWE) | Raw | Jul 6, 2026 | 122 |

Moxley leads Roman Reigns by **113 days**. Put another way: when Reigns walked out of WrestleMania 42 with the World Heavyweight Championship, Moxley had already held the Continental title for almost four months.

Look at the rest of the board and the point gets sharper. AEW crowned new world, women's world, International and TNT champions on the same night at All In on August 30. Every TNA singles title except the Knockouts Television belt changed hands at Bound for Glory on October 11. In a year when belts have moved in batches, one champion has gone the whole year without losing his.

## He isn't coasting, either

A long reign can mean a champion who rarely shows up. That isn't the case here. In the results WCN has logged since WrestleDream on October 17, Moxley beat two would-be challengers **in five days**:

- **October 17, WrestleDream:** beat Mark Davis to retain.
- **October 21, Dynamite:** beat Roderick Strong by submission in a Continental title eliminator, so Strong did not earn a title shot.

That's a pay-per-view title defense followed straight away by a TV eliminator win, against two very different opponents. Davis is a heavyweight who bounced straight back. Four nights after losing to Moxley, he and Jake Doyle beat The Young Bucks in a tag title eliminator on that same October 21 Dynamite, earning a future shot at the Bucks' AEW World Tag Team Championship. Strong is a technician who can drag a match into the ground game. Moxley got through both.

For context, WCN's records for the same stretch show title changes at WrestleDream itself, on SmackDown and on Dynamite. The Continental title held firm through all of it.

## The Death Riders problem

Here's where it gets interesting, and where the reign might be more fragile than it looks.

Moxley's faction, the Death Riders, has been far less steady than its leader. WCN logged four Death Riders team matches between October 17 and October 28:

| Date | Show | Result | Moxley in the match? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oct 17 | WrestleDream | Won (with The Dogs; Yuta pinned Colten) | No |
| Oct 21 | Dynamite | Lost to Jay White & Orange Cassidy (White pinned Yuta) | No |
| Oct 24 | Collision | Won by submission over STP | **Yes** |
| Oct 28 | Dynamite | Lost to The Cut, Jay White & David Finlay (White pinned Garcia) | **Yes** |

With Moxley in the match: **1–1**. Without him: **1–1**. And both losses came the same way, with Switchblade Jay White getting the pin, the second time with Moxley himself in the match.

That pattern matters. A champion's group losing to the same man twice in eight days is how wrestling builds a challenger. White has now pinned two different Death Riders, Wheeler Yuta and Daniel Garcia, on consecutive Dynamites. If you're keeping score, that's the clearest thread pointing at Moxley's title.

We're not saying a match has been announced. WCN only reports confirmed cards. But the story the results tell is hard to ignore.

## Why this reign deserves more credit

Long reigns get remembered when they make the belt feel more important than when it started. That's the test, and Moxley is passing it. The Continental title began life tied to a single tournament. Under Moxley, it's become a weekly measuring stick: win the Classic, then defend it against anyone, on any show.

Compare that to how the year has gone elsewhere. Liv Morgan won the Women's World Championship at Money in the Bank on October 10. The Intercontinental title went from Chad Gable to Ethan Page on November 2. Short reigns make for exciting nights. Long reigns make for history, and right now Moxley is the only active champion writing it at this length.

## What comes next

AEW Full Gear is on November 14 in Phoenix, nine days away. WWE's Crown Jewel this Saturday in Riyadh will dominate the week's headlines, and Roman Reigns, the second-longest active champion on our board, sits at the center of that card's promotion.

But if you want the reign with the most at stake per defense, watch AEW. Every week Moxley holds the title, the record gets harder to break, and every week Jay White's run against the Death Riders makes a showdown feel closer.

So here's the question heading into Full Gear: **is Jay White the one who finally ends a 300-plus-day reign, or does Moxley pass the one-year mark on December 27 with the belt still on his shoulder?**
